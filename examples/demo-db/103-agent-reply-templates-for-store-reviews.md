## Context

Store reviews are answered by whoever on the support team notices them, with no template and no record of what was said. Response time ranges from a day to never, and the public replies read inconsistently enough that people have commented on it in subsequent reviews.

## Proposal

A small set of approved reply templates and a clear rotation for who handles them.

- Six templates covering the recurring themes: crashes, missing features, pricing, sync problems, sign-in trouble, and praise
- Each template requiring at least one specific sentence, so replies are not obviously canned
- A weekly rotation with a named owner, replacing the current volunteer model
- Replies logged against the review so the history is visible to the next person

## Non-goals

No change to how reviews are solicited, which is a separate conversation with the growth team.

## Risks

Templates reduce response time and increase the chance of a reply that misses what the reviewer actually said, which in a public forum is worse than a slow reply. The required specific sentence is the control, and it should be enforced by the rotation owner rather than by hope.

## Measurement

Median response time, share of reviews receiving a reply, and rating movement among reviewers who updated after a reply.

## Data We Have

Review volume runs about ninety a month across both stores, with a long tail of one-line entries and most of the detail in the one- and two-star reviews. Sorting last quarter's by hand produced the six themes, which covered eighty-four percent of readable reviews. The rest were either unintelligible or specific to one person's situation, so templates have to coexist with free-form replies rather than replace them.

- Ninety reviews a month, roughly a third carrying enough text to answer
- Six themes cover eighty-four percent of the readable ones
- No record of which reviews were replied to, so the baseline response rate is an estimate

## Operational Impact

The weekly rotation is the real change; templates make it survivable. A named owner for a week means one person reads every review in that week, which nobody does today, and that person is first to notice when a theme stops matching the templates. Logged replies also make the history visible, so a reviewer who writes twice gets an answer that acknowledges the first one.

## What Would Change Our Mind

If rating movement after a reply comes out near zero, the case for a rotation weakens and the templates are worth keeping only for the crash and sign-in themes, where the reply carries a workaround the person can use. That measurement needs two quarters before it says anything, and the rotation should not be judged on the first.
