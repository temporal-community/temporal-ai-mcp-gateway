## Problem

Design review happens when a designer asks for it, which means it happens for work that is already finished and mostly as a courtesy. Cross-cutting inconsistencies are caught after they ship, if at all, and the review itself is a meeting where whoever is loudest shapes the outcome.

## Proposal

Two review points with different purposes and different participants.

- An early review of the approach, before visual work, focused on the problem and the pattern choice
- A later review of the result, focused on consistency with the system and with adjacent surfaces
- Written feedback before the meeting, so the meeting is for disagreement rather than for reading
- A decision recorded at each point, including when the decision is to proceed unchanged

## Non-goals

No approval gate. Review is advice, and making it a gate would route work around it.

## Risks

Two reviews is more process, and the early one is the easy one to skip when a deadline approaches, which is also when it is most valuable. If the early review is routinely skipped the later one becomes the courtesy it is today.

## Measurement

Work reviewed at each point, consistency findings raised early versus late, and designer feedback on whether the reviews are worth the time.

## Staffing

The early review needs two reviewers rather than a room: someone who knows the pattern library and someone working on an adjacent surface. The later one is wider, because consistency is a cross-surface question. Written feedback before the meeting is where the cost sits, roughly thirty minutes per reviewer per piece of work, and that cost is why the reviewer list has to stay short. A standing rotation spreads it; volunteering concentrates it on the three people who always volunteer.

## Prior Art

Engineering split technical design review the same way three years ago and kept it. What made it survive was the written document: a reviewer who has read beforehand arrives with a position, and the meeting is short enough that nobody resents it. What did not survive was the recorded decision, which lapsed within two quarters once nobody referred back to one. That is the part to watch here.

## What Would Change Our Mind

If the early review surfaces the same proportion of consistency problems as the later one, the two points are not doing different work and one should go. If designers report that the written feedback is the valuable part and the meeting adds nothing, the answer is to drop the meetings rather than add a third review point, leaving the recorded decision as the only thing the group does together.
