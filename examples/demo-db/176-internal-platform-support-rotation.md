## Problem

Questions about the shared platform arrive in a channel and are answered by whoever is free, which in practice means the two most helpful people. Their own work suffers, the knowledge stays with them, and when they are away questions sit for days.

## Proposal

A named support rotation with a defined scope and a protected time allocation.

- One person per week, with no other committed work that week
- A defined scope of what the rotation covers and what it does not, published
- Questions tracked, so recurring ones become documentation or tooling
- A handover note at the end of each week covering what is outstanding

## Non-goals

No formal response commitment to internal teams, which would turn a collegial arrangement into a contract with no funding behind it.

## Risks

Protected time is protected until a deadline arrives, and the rotation becomes a part-time duty performed badly alongside other work. The protection needs to be real at the planning level rather than stated as an intention, which means the team's commitments have to be sized with the rotation removed.

## Measurement

Questions answered within a day, the share answered by the rotation rather than by the same two people, and recurring questions converted to documentation per quarter.

## Rollout

A four-week pilot covering only the two highest-volume question areas, with the published scope saying plainly that everything else still goes to the channel. A rotation that launches claiming the whole platform gets judged on the question it was never meant to answer.

- Weeks one and two staffed by the two people absorbing the load today, so the scope document is written from what actually arrives
- Weeks three and four staffed by anyone else on the team, which is the real test of whether scope and handover are usable
- Question tracking running from week one, since the recurring-question list is the only output that compounds

## What Would Change Our Mind

If the share answered by the rotation has not moved off those two people within two months, the problem is not scheduling but genuinely concentrated knowledge, and documentation has to come before rotation. If the week consistently takes less than half the person's time, the protection costs more than it buys, and a half-week rotation with a named backup gives the same coverage.

## Open Questions

- Who covers a week during holidays, when the obvious answer is the two people this exists to protect
- Whether the handover note is written for the next person on the rotation or for the asker
- What happens to a question in scope that needs a platform change rather than an answer
