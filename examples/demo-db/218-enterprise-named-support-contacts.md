## Problem

Enterprise contracts promise a named contact. In practice tickets go to the general queue, and the named person finds out when the customer mentions them by name in a message. We are not delivering something we sell.

## Proposal

Route enterprise tickets to the named contact, with real backup when they are unavailable.

- Named contact recorded on the account and used for routing, not just stated in the contract
- A designated backup per account, briefed rather than nominal
- Response commitment tracked per account against the contract terms
- The customer able to see who their contact is, in the product

## Scope

Accounts with a contractual named contact, which is about sixty.

## Risks

Named routing concentrates load unevenly and creates a single point of failure around holidays and departures. The backup is only real if they have context, which means a regular handover rather than a name in a field, and that is ongoing work rather than a setup task.

## Measurement

Tickets handled by the named contact or briefed backup, commitment attainment per account, and account team escalations about support responsiveness.

## Staffing

Sixty accounts across the current enterprise support group is roughly eight each as primary and eight more as backup, which works only if the split follows ticket volume rather than account count. Three of the sixty generate more tickets than the bottom thirty combined. Assignment has to come from volume history and be revisited quarterly, and whoever carries the heavy accounts cannot also carry a full share of the general queue.

## Failure Modes

The quiet failure is a backup who has never handled a ticket for the account. They will be right on process and wrong on context, and the customer notices within one exchange. A handover that is a calendar entry nobody prepares for produces exactly that. The backup should take a real ticket for each account monthly even when the primary is available, which costs routing efficiency and buys a backup who is actually one.

## What Would Change Our Mind

If attainment under named routing lands below what the general queue reaches today, the contract promise and the service are in conflict and the contract is what should change. The queue has depth that one person does not. A named contact with a slower response is worse for the customer than a fast anonymous one, and we should be willing to say so rather than defend the routing because we sold it.
