## Background

The free plan limits were set at launch and have not changed. Product capability has grown considerably since, which means the free plan today is far more generous in practice than it was designed to be, and a meaningful population of workspaces have been comfortable on it for years.

## Proposal

Review the limits against their original intent and adjust deliberately, with existing workspaces protected.

- An analysis of what free workspaces actually use against each limit
- Limits reset to express the original intent in terms of current capability
- Existing workspaces grandfathered on current limits indefinitely, not for a transition period
- New limits applied only to workspaces created after the change

## Scope

The free plan. Paid plan limits are a pricing conversation and are out of scope here.

## Risks

Changing free plan limits is read, correctly, as making the free plan worse, and the reaction is public and durable regardless of how the change is framed. Grandfathering indefinitely rather than for a window is the difference between a defensible change and a broken promise, and it should not be traded away for simplicity.

## Measurement

Free to paid conversion rate for new cohorts, free workspace activity, and the volume and tone of public reaction in the first month.

## Data We Have

Usage distributions against each limit exist and have never been read with this question in mind. The shape that matters is not the median free workspace, which sits far below every limit, but the tail that has settled just under one of them and organized its work around that ceiling. Those are the workspaces a new limit would have excluded, and counting them is the difference between a limit that expresses the original intent and one that quietly redefines who the free plan is for.

## What Would Change Our Mind

If the analysis shows free workspaces clustered well under the limits with no meaningful population near any ceiling, the limits are not doing anything and resetting them buys a public argument for no effect. Leaving them alone would then be the right call. The reverse finding, a large population sitting against a single limit, is not automatically a reason to tighten it either: it may be evidence that the limit is where the product's natural boundary already is.

## Timeline

- Analysis first, with no proposed numbers attached, so the limits are argued from what workspaces do rather than from a target
- New numbers drafted and held internally while the grandfathering mechanism is built and verified against real accounts
- The published schedule and the change announced together, with the indefinite protection stated plainly rather than in a footnote
- New limits effective for workspaces created after the announcement, not after some later enablement date
