Owner: Lena Fischer
Status: In Review

## Problem

Weekend coverage is one person on a rotation, covering all channels for a volume that is about forty percent of a weekday. Response times on Saturday are three times the weekday figure, and the person on duty describes the shift as unmanageable rather than quiet.

## Proposal

Size weekend coverage to weekend volume and narrow what it commits to.

- Two people for the peak six hours rather than one across twelve
- A published scope: account access, payment failures, and outages, with everything else deferred
- Clear expectation setting at submission, so customers know what will wait
- Monday morning staffed for the deferred backlog rather than treated as a normal start

## Non-goals

No move to follow-the-sun coverage, which is a hiring decision rather than a scheduling one.

## Risks

Publishing a narrow weekend scope is an explicit reduction of a service people currently receive inconsistently, and some customers will read it as a downgrade even though it describes what already happens. The alternative is continuing to imply a coverage we do not provide.

## Measurement

Weekend response times within the published scope, Monday backlog size, and weekend shift feedback from the people working it.

## Data We Have

Twelve months of weekend volume by hour, which is where the six-hour peak comes from.

- Saturday concentrates between late morning and mid-afternoon in the two largest regions, about sixty percent of the day inside six hours
- Sunday is flatter and smaller, so the same window fits worse and the peak hours have to differ by day
- Contact reason is recorded, so the share falling inside the proposed scope looks like roughly a third

What we cannot see is what the deferred two-thirds cost on Monday. Monday volume exists, and weekend backlog cannot be separated from normal Monday arrivals, so that measure has to be instrumented before the change rather than after.

## Timeline

Publishing the scope is the slow step and starts first, since the wording has to be accepted by support, legal, and the account teams. The two-person peak schedule can go ahead before that, because it improves response times without promising anything new. Monday staffing follows the first month of backlog measurement instead of being guessed.

## What Would Change Our Mind

If the in-scope share is well under a third, the narrow scope defers more than it covers, and the honest shape is a longer window with a wider scope and a slower commitment. If shift feedback does not improve with two people at the peak, the problem is the span of channels one person holds rather than the volume per hour, and this proposal does not address that.
