## Context

Enterprise customers ask for a phone number. We do not have one. The requests are concentrated in two segments and come up in roughly a quarter of renewal conversations, usually as a nice-to-have rather than a blocker, but consistently.

## Proposal

A bounded pilot for enterprise accounts only, measured properly before any expansion.

- Scheduled calls rather than an inbound line, which bounds the staffing requirement
- An AI transcription and summarization pass so the call produces a ticket record rather than a memory
- Available to accounts above a contract threshold, booked from the account view
- A fixed pilot duration with a decision at the end, including the option to stop

## Scope

Twenty accounts for one quarter.

## Risks

Voice is the most expensive channel per contact and the hardest to staff, and a pilot that succeeds creates an expectation that is difficult to withdraw. The exit criteria should be written before the pilot starts. Transcription accuracy on technical vocabulary and on accented speech varies considerably, and a wrong transcript that becomes the ticket record is worse than no record, so the agents on the call must review before it is saved.

## Measurement

Calls booked and held, resolution rate against the equivalent ticket path, cost per contact, transcript corrections per call, and account team feedback at the quarter's end.

## Staffing

Twenty accounts for a quarter is roughly four scheduled calls a week, which sounds like nothing and is not. Scheduled calls have to be covered at a fixed hour by someone who can answer without escalating, which means two senior support staff carrying the slot rather than one, since one person cannot hold a booked hour across a quarter without a backup. The review of the transcript before it is saved adds ten to fifteen minutes per call on top of the call itself.

- Two senior support staff cover the booking slots, with a named backup each week
- Calls limited to a published window so the slot does not spread across the day
- The reviewer of the transcript is the person who took the call, not a second queue

## Cost

Cost per contact here is dominated by the held hour, not by telephony or by the transcription pass. A thirty-minute call with preparation and transcript review lands near ninety minutes of senior time, against a handful of minutes for the equivalent ticket. The pilot is worth running at that price because it is bounded; the same price across the enterprise base is the decision the pilot exists to inform.

## What Would Change Our Mind

If resolution rate on calls matches the ticket path rather than beating it, the channel is a preference and should be priced as one rather than absorbed. If transcript corrections stay high on technical vocabulary through the quarter, the record half of this proposal does not work and the call reverts to notes typed by the person who took it, which changes the cost per contact again.
