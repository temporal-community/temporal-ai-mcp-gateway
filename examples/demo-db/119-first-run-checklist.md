## Context

New workspaces that complete three specific actions in their first week retain at roughly four times the rate of those that do not. We know which three actions. We do nothing to encourage them beyond a welcome email that about a fifth of people open.

## Proposal

A checklist in the product showing the three actions and tracking completion.

- Three items only, chosen from the retention analysis rather than from what we want to promote
- Visible on the main surface until complete or dismissed, then gone permanently
- Each item deep-links into the action rather than into documentation about it
- Completion state per workspace, not per person, since the actions are workspace-level

## Non-goals

No gamification, no progress celebration, and no expansion of the list to cover features that would like attention.

## Risks

The correlation between those three actions and retention is not evidence that causing the actions causes retention. Workspaces that do them may simply be more committed. A holdout is the only way to know, and it should be sized to detect a realistic effect rather than the one we hope for.

## Measurement

Checklist completion rate, week-four retention against a holdout, and dismissal rate, which tells us whether it is useful or merely tolerated.

## Data We Have

The four-times figure comes from twelve months of workspace cohorts and holds across plan tiers, which is the strongest thing about it. What we do not have is the order those three actions usually happen in, or how long a workspace takes between the first and the third. Both matter for whether a list of three is the right shape or whether the items should arrive one at a time. We also have no count of workspaces that already complete all three unprompted; if that is most of them, the addressable population is far smaller than the retention gap implies and the holdout will take a long time to say anything.

## Interfaces Affected

- The main surface, which gains a persistent element and therefore an argument about what it displaces
- Three deep links into the actions, each of which has to behave in a workspace with no content in it yet
- Workspace-level state, since completion is shared and one person's dismissal dismisses it for everyone
- The welcome email, which should link to the checklist rather than restate the same three actions in different words

## What Would Change Our Mind

If the holdout shows completion rising while week-four retention stays flat, the correlation was selection, the checklist is decoration, and it should be removed rather than iterated on. That result would also mean these three actions stop being treated as levers in other plans. A dismissal rate above roughly a third says something different and worse, that the element is simply in the way, and that is a reason to change the surface rather than abandon the premise.
