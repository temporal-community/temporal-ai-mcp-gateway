## Problem

Internal notes on tickets are written for the person writing them. They contain shorthand, half-sentences, and occasionally opinions about the customer that would be embarrassing in a data request. For a ticket that passes between three people, the notes are the context, and they are frequently useless.

## Proposal

A short convention for notes and a reminder of who might read them.

- A three-line structure: what was tried, what is known, what is next
- A visible reminder that notes are disclosable in a data request, because many people do not know this
- A review of existing notes for content that should not be retained, with a retention period applied going forward
- Notes quality included in the existing quality review rather than measured separately

## Non-goals

No prohibition on candor. Notes should be useful and professional, not sanitized into uselessness.

## Risks

Telling people their notes are disclosable will make some stop writing them, which would be worse than the current state. The framing has to be about writing for a colleague rather than about legal exposure, with the disclosure point made once.

## Measurement

Notes meeting the structure in the quality sample, handoffs requiring the customer to repeat information, and retention compliance on the historical review.

## Rollout

The three-line structure goes first and alone, framed as a handoff aid. The disclosure reminder follows a month later, once the structure is a habit, because leading with disclosure makes the structure look like a compliance measure rather than a way to help the next person.

- Week one: the structure, with three worked examples from real tickets, shown in the note field rather than in a document
- Week five: the disclosure line, shown once per person rather than on every note
- Week eight: notes folded into the existing quality sample at the same weight as other criteria

## Operational Impact

Writing three lines takes longer than writing shorthand: a few seconds per note across every ticket. That cost is paid by the person writing while the benefit lands on whoever picks the ticket up, so teams whose tickets rarely change hands feel only the cost and the structure should be expected of them rather than enforced. The historical review is the larger item, a read of years of notes by someone with authority to redact, and nobody owns it today.

## Open Questions

- Whether retention runs from the note or from the ticket closing, which differ by months on long cases
- Whether the historical review redacts in place or deletes, since a redacted note still shows something was removed
- What the structure does for a note that is genuinely one line, given forcing three invites filler
- Who decides a note crosses the line, which the quality sample then has to apply consistently
